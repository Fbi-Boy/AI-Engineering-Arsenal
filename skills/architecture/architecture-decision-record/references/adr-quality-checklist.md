# ADR Review Checklist

## Context

- Does the record explain the problem and why a decision is needed?
- Can someone outside the original discussion understand the context?
- Are important requirements, quality attributes, constraints, and assumptions stated?
- Are sources or evidence linked?

## Options

- Are the alternatives viable and fairly described?
- Is the status quo considered when reasonable?
- Are pros, cons, costs, risks, migration, and operating implications visible?
- Are benchmark or prototype claims supported by evidence?
- Are scoring weights and methods explained if used?

## Decision

- Is one decision stated clearly?
- Does the language distinguish an accepted decision from a recommendation?
- Is the decision owner or approving forum identified?
- Does the rationale connect to project needs and constraints?

## Consequences

- Are positive and negative consequences both described?
- Are new risks and obligations acknowledged?
- Are follow-up actions, owners, and monitoring needs recorded where known?
- Is reversibility or exit strategy considered?
- Are revisit triggers explicit?

## Lifecycle and repository hygiene

- Is the status accurate?
- Are dates and authors present?
- Does the file follow repository naming and numbering conventions?
- Are related and superseding ADRs linked?
- Is an accepted record preserved rather than silently rewritten?
- Is the content safe to publish in the target repository?

## Review outcome

Use one of:
- Accept — required context, decision authority, and trade-offs are clear.
- Request changes — material information is missing or misleading.
- Keep Proposed — decision authority or evidence is not yet available.

Do not infer an approval from the existence of an ADR file or pull request.
